Getting around

All the numbers are in the site's own Grafana: grafana.example.com (behind Cloudflare's protection if the site is), or grafana.<home domain> at home, in a folder named after the site. Sign in with the account whoever runs the site made for you. Everything here is read-only: looking around can't break or change anything. For the short version without opening Grafana, your phone gets a summary of the week every Monday morning (Posting Guide).

Moving between dashboards

Every dashboard has the same links along the top, in the same places: Overview, Health, Traffic, Growth, Members, Content, Social and Platforms (Social and Platforms only when the site shares its posts: the social module). The line under the title has them too, with the one you're on in bold. Both keep your time range, so "last 7 days" on Traffic is still "last 7 days" when you open Growth. Two more aren't in that list: Post opens when you click a post (see One post up close), and Page when you click a page on the site (see Every post about one page).

Hover a chart and a line follows your mouse across every chart on that dashboard, so you can see what else happened on the same day.

To jump to a section, for example Instagram on Platforms, click Outline (the icon with lines, in the thin bar on the right edge). It lists the dashboard's sections, and clicking one scrolls straight to it. On Platforms the site comes first, then the networks in A-Z order, and the When to post tables list them A-Z too. Networks that aren't set up aren't on the page, so they aren't listed either.

The time range

The time picker at the top right (for example "Last 30 days") sets the time range for most panels. A panel whose title says selected range follows it, while all time, now or last 30 days panels ignore it. The networks' own totals on Platforms mostly cover the last 30 days, whatever the time range.

  • Pick a few days or more for the daily charts: each bar or point is one day. Under a day, Ghost counts in hours and the numbers mean something slightly different (see Unique visitors).
  • Compare with before: Members puts "New members (previous period)" right next to the selected range's number, e.g. the 30 days before the last 30.
  • Dashboards refresh themselves (Health every minute, the rest every 5 minutes), so you can leave one open.

Explanations and markers

  • Every panel explains itself. Hover the i next to a title for what it counts and where the number comes from. This guide covers the main points; the i has the details.
  • Post markers: on Overview, Traffic and Growth, dotted lines show what you posted: magenta = a post on the site (Ghost), violet = a social post. Hover the little triangle at the bottom of a line to see which post. Switch them off with the toggles at the top of the dashboard.
  • Tables: click a column heading to sort by it. On Growth, the funnel icon on a heading filters that column.
  • Dropdowns inside a section: some sections have their own dropdowns at the top (sort, which number to compare on, which source). They only affect that section.
Start with Overview

Its Alerts firing now box at the top lists anything that's wrong right now: the same alerts that reach your phone through ntfy. If it's empty, everything's working and the numbers can be trusted.

Common questions

Did my post work?

  1. The quickest answer is the two verdict columns, vs your usual (against your own posts) and vs industry (against the published average for that network): in every posts table on Platforms, in Recently published on Social, and in Best posts lately on Overview. Hover either for why. See Is it doing well? for what they're based on.
  2. On Growth, look at What moved your traffic. A jump in visitors or members right after a marker means that post is working.
  3. On Platforms, each network's charts have a violet line for every post on that network, so you can see whether followers, views, plays or engagement jumped after it. The site's charts (The site (Ghost)) has them all: magenta for site posts, violet for every network's, with the network named in the popup. Hover a line to see what went out.
  4. To see when in the day it happened, set the time range (top right) to 7 days or less, e.g. Last 24 hours: the charts switch from per day to hour by hour by themselves (their titles say which). Per day says whether a day went up; hour by hour says when: a step up an hour or two after a post is that post bringing people in. Hour by hour shows each of the bridge's checks, about one an hour, with the same post lines: the followers as they stood (subscribers on YouTube, members on the site), and on the other chart what came in since the check before (views, plays, visitors, likes and the rest), without the 7-day lines. It keeps the last 90 days. YouTube's minutes watched are only per day (YouTube gives them two or three days late).
  5. For a social post, open Platforms, find the network's posts table and click the post's row. The Post dashboard compares it with your usual post on that network and shows what it did for the site (One post up close).
  6. For an Instagram Story, see Platforms → Instagram → Instagram posts & Stories: Stories are listed with the posts, Type Story, with their views, reach, replies, taps, and the follows and profile visits they brought. Instagram keeps a Story's numbers only while it's up, so the bridge saves them while it can; Stories posted before that, or up for less than an hour, aren't listed. Click one for the Post dashboard, like a post: it's compared with your other Stories, not your posts.
  7. For a post on the site, open Platforms → The site (Ghost) and click its row in Ghost posts, or click the link in a magenta marker's popup. The Post dashboard compares its visitors with your usual site post at the same age and shows where they came from. Content → Post performance (visitors, comments, signups) and Growth's Content table rank all posts side by side.

Where do visitors come from?

Traffic → Top sources gives the plain list. Growth goes further:

  • Top network: the network that sent the most visitors, with all its links added together (Instagram's many ways in counted as one).
  • Sources: visitors per source, next to how many of them joined.
  • Tagged links, by network and link: your own links, split into posts and bio links. These count even when an app hides where people came from, which Instagram and TikTok often do.
  • One source up close: pick a source in that section's dropdown to see its visitors per day next to your post markers, and what its visitors read. This shows whether posting on a network brings people from it, and for how long.

What turns visitors into members?

Visitor → member at the top of Growth is the number to grow: more visitors only help if some of them join. Then look at:

  • Sources: a source with few visitors but many signups for its size is worth more of your time.
  • Content: what people read, and what makes them join: posts that bring members are the ones to make more of. A signup counts for the page the person joined on.
  • Members → Where signups came from and Signups from posts.
  • For one page that brought members, click it (its path in Growth's Content table, or its row in Content's Top pages) for the Page dashboard: every post that sent people there, and which networks its new members came from (Every post about one page).

To find out which of your own links works best, tag it: a QR code on a flyer, an ad or a special post can carry a campaign and then gets its own row in Growth's Campaigns table. For a post, add the internal tag #campaign-<name> in Ghost (Posting Guide).

Which network is worth my time?

Look at two things together. Platforms shows how each network's own audience reacts (followers, views, plays). Growth shows which networks send people to the site and turn them into members. A network can be big on its own numbers and send nobody, or small and bring members: both matter, for different reasons.

On Instagram, two numbers sit in between: Profile views (people curious enough to open your profile) and Link taps (taps on the links on it, your example.com bio link among them). Both are on Platforms → Instagram, per day. Instagram counts taps on all your profile's links together; the visits your bio link brought are in Growth's Tagged links (Instagram, bio link). Many profile views but few link taps: the profile isn't sending people on.

Threads has its own in-between number: Link clicks, taps on links in your posts, on its reach & engagement chart on Platforms → Threads. To compare networks with each other, hover each network's reach & engagement chart for its engagement rate: how many of the people who saw your posts also reacted to them (Daily lines, 7-day lines and engagement). On YouTube, Where YouTube views come from (beside its per-day chart) splits the last 28 days' views by how people found them: YouTube search, suggested videos, the Shorts feed, other sites and so on.

Who follows me, and when are they online?

Platforms → Instagram, below the posts & Stories: your followers by country, by age and by gender, and When followers are online, the average number on Instagram in each hour of the day (the site's time zone). Posting an hour or so before the busiest hours gives a post the best start. Instagram gives these only from 100 followers, and checks them once a day.

When should I post?

Platforms → When to post, near the bottom. It needs some reading: see When to post. For Instagram, also look at When followers are online in its section (Who follows me).

How's the newsletter doing?

Members: the Newsletters table lists the last 25 sends with delivery, opens, unsubscribes in the 2 days after and spam complaints. Email health below it shows whether the list is growing (net subscriptions), delivery problems and members Ghost has stopped emailing. Open rates read high: see Newsletter opens.

Do people click through to my work elsewhere?

Content → Links people follow off the site: clicks to YouTube, Vimeo, Twitch, Patreon, Substack, Etsy, Spotify, SoundCloud, the social networks and the rest, each by name (anything else is Other), which pages they came from and where they went. Plays inside a player embedded in a post can't be seen from the site; those are in each service's own numbers on Platforms.

How did my live show go?

Content → Live shows: each show's length, the most listeners at once, the average, how many times people pressed play on the LIVE NOW bar, how often listeners' players paused to buffer or reconnected, and the setlist (the track titles your streaming software sent). While you're on air, Health shows On air and Listeners now.

Listeners heard gaps? Health → Live stream: listeners' pauses and reconnects shows each one and why. If many listeners paused at the same moment, look at Live stream: coming in from the streaming software: a dip there means the sound left home unevenly (the streaming software or the home internet), not a listener problem. One pause now and then is that listener's own connection or device, and the table beside it says which (phone or computer, browser, and whether the page was in the background). Live stream: skipped for slow listeners should stay at zero.

Reading the numbers right

Unique visitors

The site counts visitors without cookies, which is kinder to visitors but means it can't recognise someone the next day. So Unique visitors adds up each day's visitors: someone who comes on 3 days counts 3 times. This is the same number Ghost Admin shows. Unique visitors per day on Traffic (the total divided by the days) is the closest it gets to "how many people".

Because one network turns up under several names, Growth's Top network adds them up, so its number won't match any single row of the Sources table. Growth can also hide the Direct row and the homepage with the Hide/Show dropdowns in those sections, to make the rest easier to compare.

Posts vs bio links

Every link the social sharing puts in a post is tagged as a post link (for example example.com/b/… on Bluesky). Your profile short links (example.com/ig, /bsky, /th, /tk, /dis, /yt) are tagged as bio links. Growth's Tagged links and Posts vs bio links show which way people came in: through a post, or by visiting your profile.

Days and time zones

The site's own daily charts use days in the site's time zone. Numbers the networks give per day use UTC days, which turn over at a different hour. A post late in the evening can therefore land on "tomorrow" in some charts.

Newsletter opens

Apple Mail opens every email by itself to protect privacy, so those count as opens and open rates read high. Compare newsletters with each other rather than with outside benchmarks. "Unsubscribed (2 days)" counts anyone who unsubscribed in the 2 days after a send, so it's a hint, not proof that this newsletter was the reason.

Site visitors vs Cloudflare's requests

Traffic's Everything Cloudflare answered section counts every request, including bots, feeds and search engines, so it's far higher than the visitor numbers. Use the visitor numbers for people. Cloudflare's are there to spot unusual traffic.

Daily lines, 7-day lines and engagement

Each network's section on Platforms has a followers chart and a reach & engagement chart (Instagram also has profile views & link taps). On a small account one day can be twice the next for no real reason, so every per-day number is drawn twice: a thin dashed line for each day, and a bold line for its 7-day average, the trend to read. The bold line starts once there are 4 days to average.

  • Reach, views or plays (the network's colour, left scale): how many saw or played something of yours. Instagram counts accounts reached, TikTok, Threads and YouTube views, SoundCloud and Mixcloud plays.
  • Engagement (blue, right scale): likes, comments, shares and saves added up, in each network's own words (favorites and reposts on Mixcloud, replies and quotes on Threads and Bluesky). On the site's chart (Site visitors & engagement) it's what visitors did: new members, comments, clicks on links to other sites and plays on the LIVE NOW bar. Hover for each one. It has its own scale because it's usually a small part of the reach: if the two lines move together, people react about as much as before; engagement rising faster means posts are landing better, slower means more people see them but fewer join in.
  • Engagement rate, 7-day (in the hover popup): the week's engagement as a share of the week's reach, views or plays, so it can be compared between networks and with the typical rate the popup names. Bluesky gives no views, so its chart shows engagement per 100 followers instead; the site's is per 100 visitors.

Hour by hour (time ranges of 7 days or less) shows what came in at each check, with no 7-day lines and no rate: those only make sense per day. On Instagram and Threads, the hour-by-hour engagement is what all your posts gained since the last check, because those networks' own per-day totals arrive late and would look flat; so it can differ a little from the per-day line.

1.5× and 0.5×

The Post dashboard and When to post compare with your usual: 1× = usual, 1.5× = half again as much, 2× = double, 0.5× = half. "Usual" is the median: the middle post, so one viral hit doesn't move it much.

One post up close

The Post dashboard looks at a single post: a social one, or one on the site. You open it by clicking:

  • a row in a posts table on Platforms (the site's own posts are in The site (Ghost)),
  • a row in Recently published on Social, or in Best posts lately on Overview,
  • or the link in a marker's popup on Overview, Traffic or Growth (magenta for a site post, violet for a social one).

The time range starts when the post went up, so everything on the page is "since posting". A violet line marks the moment, and the post's own numbers are in its network's colour, as on Platforms. The links at the top take you back to the other dashboards on their usual time range, not the post's.

On the network

  • Now: its numbers as last collected (every hour, or as often as the bridge is set to check). Instagram posts also list the follows and profile visits the post brought and its interactions (likes, comments, shares and saves together); Bluesky posts their saves.
  • Number by number vs your usual post: each number against your typical post on the same network at the same age, so a new post isn't held against posts that have had weeks. In a post's first 4 days that's to the hour (5 hours after posting against others at 5 hours), from every check the bridge keeps then; after that, by days since posting. It needs at least 3 other posts measured at that age; when another post wasn't checked on exactly that day, its nearest day is used (a day either side for posts under a week old, two under four weeks, three after that). Until there are 3, the table shows only a line saying why it's empty, and the chart beside it has no "typical" line.
  • The chart: this post's total day by day next to your typical post's. Change the number it shows with the Compare on dropdown in that section.
  • Gained per day and Totals over time. Totals over time shows every check the bridge has made of the post since it first saw it: all of them in its first 4 days, so you see the curve of its first hours, and after that every time a number changed. An older post that suddenly takes off (the bridge's take-off watch, below) has all its checks kept again for a few days; the note above the chart says when.
  • Take-off watch: the bridge keeps an eye on every older post's main number. When one gains, within about the last 4 hours or the last 18, at least 5% of what it had (and at least 10), and at least 3 times its usual gain from the week before, it keeps every check of that post for 3 days, longer while it keeps climbing. Instagram and Threads then check that post every time instead of taking turns, and your phone gets a "📈 … is taking off" notification with a button to the post's Post dashboard.

Is it doing well?

Every post gets two verdicts, side by side at the top of the network section and as two columns everywhere posts are listed:

  • vs your usual (on the Post dashboard: vs your other <network> posts): Doing great, Above usual, Usual, Below usual or Well below usual, against your own posts, and by how much: "Doing great · better than 9 in 10" means it beat about 9 of every 10 posts it was compared with.
  • vs industry (vs the industry average): Well above typical, Above typical, About typical, Below typical or Well below typical, against the published average for that network, with both numbers: "Well above typical · 7.4% vs 3.2%".

vs your usual is worked out the way people who study social media numbers recommend:

  • Against your own posts, not other people's. Each post is compared with your 20 posts nearest in time on the same network (only the same kind, e.g. Reels with Reels or Shorts with Shorts, when there are 5 of that kind). Nearest in time, so a growing account doesn't make every new post look great.
  • On what each network says it rewards. Every network picks which posts to show more of from a few signals; those are what's measured, with the most important counting most (the weights are in the table on the Post dashboard).
  • Per person reached, not raw counts. 4 likes from 50 people who saw it beats 40 from 5,000. Counts like reach, views and plays are compared at the same age (days since posting), since they keep growing for days.
  • Small numbers count for less. 2 likes out of 6 views isn't "33%, amazing": a rate from few people is pulled toward your usual one.
  • As a ranking. Doing great = better overall than about 4 in 5 of the posts it's compared with; Usual = the middle fifth. With only a few posts to compare with it stays nearer Usual, because it can't be sure yet.

What each verdict means:

  • About typical: between two thirds and one and a half times the average, or inside its range (for Instagram's 3.2%: about 2.1% to 4.8%).
  • Above typical, Below typical: outside that band.
  • Well above typical, Well below typical: twice the band's top or more, or under half its bottom (for Instagram: from 9.6%, or under 1.1%).
  • Too few to tell: fewer than 50 people reached, views or recipients.
  • Too soon: posted less than 12 hours ago; rates swing a lot before then.
  • No numbers yet: the network hasn't given the numbers it needs (reach, views, likes and so on); it fills in after the next check that gets them.
  • Not a newsletter (the site): the post wasn't emailed.
  • No benchmark: Bluesky, Threads, SoundCloud, Mixcloud and Instagram Stories have no reliable published average.

Hover the cell, or look at the Post dashboard's Why, for the reason in words. Socialinsider's headline numbers (Instagram 0.45%, TikTok 2.6%) are per follower, a different measure, so they're not used here. Keep in mind those averages come mostly from big brand accounts; small, engaged accounts often beat them, so vs your usual says more about what's working for you.

Not enough posts yet / Too soon

A post gets a verdict once there are 5 other posts on the same network to compare it with. In its first 4 days it's compared hour for hour with what those posts had at the same age, from 2 hours after posting; that needs 5 posts with checks from their own first days, which the bridge keeps from the day it started. Until there are 5 such posts, a new post is left alone for its first 12 hours, then compared day by day. Counts like views also need each post's numbers per day, kept from the bridge's first day too (YouTube's and the site's go back further), so until those build up, posts are judged mostly on their rates.

What it did for the site

The bottom section looks at the page on example.com the post leads to: its visitors, how many came from this network, and the members who joined there. Its title names the page.

For a post on the site, the page is the post itself. Its own numbers are its visitors and page views per day, the members who signed up on it and, if it went out as a newsletter, its opens and clicks. "…of them from the newsletter" counts the visitors and members who came from the email's links.

Instagram and TikTok captions can't carry links, so for those the page is a guess: the site post whose title the caption mentions, otherwise the newest site post from the week before. If the guess is wrong, pick the right page in that section's page dropdown (your post's other links, the homepage and the latest site posts are listed). "-" means no page.

To see every other post that led to the same page, click the Visitors to the page per day chart's title and pick Every post about this page: that opens the Page dashboard, below.

Every post about one page

The Page dashboard is the Post dashboard the other way round: pick one page on example.com and it shows every post, on every network, that led to it, and what they did for it together. It opens from a page's path or title in Content's Post performance (the second link) and Top pages, Growth's Content table, and the Post dashboard's Visitors to the page per day, keeping your time range. You can also pick a page in its page dropdown at the top (the latest site posts and the homepage).

  • The tiles: how many social posts led here, the page's visitors in the time range, how many of them came from a network or the newsletter, and the members who joined on the page.
  • Posts that led here: every one, oldest first, with its network, its main number (views, plays and so on) and its vs your usual verdict. Links here means the post links to the page; Instagram and TikTok can't carry links, so theirs are the same guesses as on the Post dashboard (shown in orange). The site post itself is listed as The post itself. Click a row for that post's Post dashboard.
  • Visitors to the page per day, with a violet line for each of those posts (hover for which), so you can see which one brought a jump.
  • Visitors by network and Members who joined there, by network: where the page's visitors and new members came from, each network's ways in added up as on Growth. Several posts on the same network can't be told apart in the site's numbers, so these are per network, not per post.

When to post

At the bottom of Platforms, two tables (by weekday and by time of day) show how posts made at each time do against your average, one row per network. A third, what kind of post, shows how each kind (Reel or photo, Short or video, track or mix...) does against your average kind, one row per network and kind, for the networks where you post more than one kind.

Reading a cell

  • The number: 1.3× on Friday means Friday posts do 30% better than your average weekday. Green from 1.1×, red under 0.9×. An empty cell had no posts.
  • How sure it is: plain = High (at least 95% likely really better or worse), a little faded = Medium (80%+), faded and striped = Low (could easily be luck; always the case with a single post). With many cells in a table, one could look sure by chance alone, so the bridge also shuffles your posts' times (or kinds) 200 times and sees how sure the surest cell gets then: High has to beat 95 in every 100 of those shuffled tables, Medium 80, so a cell isn't High just by luck. Right after the numbers change this takes a moment; until then the plain 95% / 80% apply (the Posts tooltip says which).
  • Details: the small triangle in a cell's corner opens its tooltip: how many posts, their plain median, the percentage, and how often shuffled posts do as well.

What it takes into account

It tries hard not to give a slot credit it doesn't deserve:

  • Each post is judged by its numbers 3 days after posting, so old posts don't win just by having been up longer. Posts younger than 3 days aren't in yet.
  • It weighs weekday, time of day, the kind of post (Reel or photo, Short or video, track or mix, a site post sent as a newsletter or not) and the account's growth over time together. If your Friday posts were also mostly Reels, Friday doesn't get the Reels' credit; the what kind of post table shows that side of it. Kinds counted: Instagram and Threads by type, YouTube Short, Video or Live, SoundCloud track or mix (20 minutes or more), Bluesky images, video, link or text (quotes count as text), TikTok short video, long video (a minute or more) or photo, Mixcloud clip or full mix (30 minutes or more), site post with or without the newsletter.
  • Cells with few posts are pulled toward 1×, and a one-off hit counts for less.

Each network is measured on its main number (visitors for the site, views on Instagram, Threads, TikTok and YouTube, plays on SoundCloud and Mixcloud, likes on Bluesky). To use another, pick it in that network's dropdown at the top of the section. Times are in the site's time zone.

Hints, not rules

It's based on all your posts on each network that are at least 3 days old. Trust High cells, take Medium ones as a nudge, and treat Low ones as "not enough posts to say yet". The more you post at different times, the more it can tell you.

What the numbers can't tell you

  • How many different people visited over a week or a month: without cookies, the same person on different days counts again (Unique visitors).
  • Plays inside an embedded player (a SoundCloud or YouTube player in a post): the site can't see inside it. They're in that service's own numbers.
  • Where every visitor came from: apps often hide it, which is why your links carry tags. Untagged visits from apps end up as Direct / unknown.
  • Old posts' history: the networks mostly give a post's numbers as of now, so the day-by-day history starts when the collecting began. YouTube and the site are the exceptions: YouTube's history goes back to each upload, the site's to when its analytics started (a post's visitors from before then aren't counted).
  • Bluesky likes, reposts and replies per day before the collecting began: Bluesky gives no numbers per day, so Platforms adds up what each of your posts gained between the bridge's checks. A post the bridge first saw a day or more after it went up starts counting from then.
  • Which link on your Instagram profile was tapped: Instagram counts taps on all of them together. Profile views and link taps go back 30 days from when the collecting began.
  • Up-to-the-hour numbers for older Instagram and Threads posts: Platforms lists every post, but those two networks need a separate request for each post's views and reach, so older posts are refreshed in turns and can be a few hours behind. Likes and comments on Instagram are always current.
  • Hour by hour more than 90 days back: the hour-by-hour view (Platforms' charts with a time range of 7 days or less) keeps 90 days. Per day goes back further. The first check of a day, or one after a gap, can show no point: there's nothing before it to compare with. A network refreshes its own counts only so often (YouTube rounds subscribers, TikTok updates every few hours), so some lines move in steps whatever the bridge does.
  • Deleted posts: they leave the posts tables. What was collected about them stays, and When to post still counts them.
  • Instagram's new followers per day while the account has under 100 followers: Instagram doesn't give them.
  • The last 2 to 3 days of Google Search: Google hasn't released them yet.

Collected, not shown yet. The dashboards show only part of what's collected. Everything else each network offers is kept too, so a chart can be added later with its past already there. That includes the rest of each post's numbers, Instagram's daily reach split into followers and non-followers, who follows you on Threads and who Instagram reaches beyond your followers, YouTube viewers' countries, devices and ages, the searches and pages Google showed the site for each day (Google itself keeps only 16 months) and Postiz's numbers for every connected channel. A new chart is a change to the kit itself (changes made in Grafana don't stay): to ask for one, open an issue on the kit's GitHub page.

Something looks wrong?

A network's section is missing from Platforms.
It has no numbers yet: not set up, or every try so far has failed. The Networks not shown box at the bottom lists the hidden ones with the reason. What each network needs: the Setup guide (for most of them, connecting the channel in Postiz and allowing every permission is all it takes).
A network's numbers look old, or its status panel shows an error.
Each section has a status panel saying when the numbers were last collected and what went wrong. Often a channel needs reconnecting in Postiz. If they stop updating, an alert ("… numbers stopped updating") comes to your phone too.
The site's posts on Platforms have no visitors.
Their status panel says why. "needs TINYBIRD_TOKEN" means the bridge can't read the site's visitor counts, usually because the analytics module is off: tell whoever runs the site. A brand-new post gets its first numbers within the hour.
The charts are empty or show a single bar.
Pick a longer time range: daily charts need a few days.
"Number by number vs your usual" says there isn't enough to compare with.
It needs 3 other posts on that network measured at the same age. It fills in as newer posts get older.
The Post dashboard's site section is empty, or shows the wrong page.
No page was found or the guess was wrong: pick the page in that section's page dropdown.
A post I made by hand on Instagram shows on Social as published, but not in Postiz.
That's expected: Social reads Instagram's own account, since posts you finish in the app never come back to Postiz.
Visitors suddenly dropped to zero.
Check Alerts firing now on Overview: "Visitors aren't being counted" or "no page views since yesterday" means the counting broke, not that people stopped coming. Tell whoever runs the site.
"Your own site" shows up as a source.
That's mostly you, from Ghost Admin or previews. Ignore it.

Glossary

Grafana
The dashboards, at the site's Grafana address.
Unique visitors
People who visited, counted once per day and added up over the time range.
Pageviews
Pages opened. One visitor reading three posts is three pageviews.
Bounce rate
The share of visits that saw only one page.
Member
Someone signed up to the site (free or paid). Members get the newsletter unless they turn it off.
Visitor → member
New members divided by unique visitors: how much of your traffic joins.
Source
Where a visit came from: another site or app, or one of your tags.
Tag (in a link)
A label added to a link (?utm_source=…) saying which network and kind of link it is, so a visit is counted right even when the app hides where it came from.
Bio link
A short link like example.com/ig for a profile.
Campaign
A name added to a link's tag, for links you want counted on their own (a flyer, an ad, a #campaign-… post).
Median / usual
The middle value: half your posts did better, half worse. One viral post barely moves it.
Reach
On Instagram, how many accounts saw anything of yours.
Engagement
Likes, comments, shares and saves added up: how many times people reacted, not just saw.
Engagement rate
Engagement as a share of reach, views or plays over 7 days, so networks can be compared.
7-day average
The bold line on Platforms' charts: each day's number averaged with the 6 days before it, to show the trend.
Marker
A dotted line on a chart: magenta = a site post, violet = a social post.
The bridge
The program that shares site posts to social media and also collects the networks' numbers (every hour unless set to check more often) and keeps every change.
