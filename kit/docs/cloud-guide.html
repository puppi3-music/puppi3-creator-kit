How it goes

The site keeps running where it is, on Docker Compose. For the busy stretch, a second folder of the same site goes on a cluster in the cloud with k8s/up.sh, and k8s/migrate/ carries the data across: Ghost and its database, the analytics, the stream server, Postiz, the bridge, and the site's own Grafana and Prometheus with their history. In the cloud, the databases are the cloud's own (RDS or Cloud SQL), and Ghost's pictures and files go in a bucket.

  1. Once, now: Cloudflare's cache. It helps at home too, and it's what keeps a busy stretch cheap.
  2. Once per cloud: the account, the cluster, the databases, the bucket and the permissions (EKS or GKE), then the cluster's folder. Budget an afternoon the first time.
  3. A day or so before: make the cluster, bring the empty site up and try it, while the Compose site keeps serving.
  4. The move, about half an hour: freeze, to-cloud.sh, check, point the DNS at the cloud (The move).
  5. Afterwards: to-nas.sh, the DNS back home (Coming home), then delete what costs money.
One copy at a time

Postiz, Temporal and the bridge run in one place only: two bridges would share every post twice. to-cloud.sh stops them on the Compose side, and they stay stopped until to-nas.sh starts them again there. Don't start that side with kit/kit.sh … up while the site is in the cloud.

The scripts never touch your DNS (both switches are yours, by hand), never make or delete anything in the cloud (the commands below do, and you run them), and never go ahead without asking: they show what they'll do and wait for a yes, and with -n they only check and print the steps.

Cloudflare's cache first

Ghost sends its pages with Cache-Control: public, max-age=0, so every visit to a post reaches your server. A cache rule lets Cloudflare answer visitors who aren't signed in for a few minutes at a time, so a busy night barely reaches the site, wherever it runs, and a cloud's outgoing traffic (which costs money) drops. Pictures and other files under /content/ are cached already. For a site behind Cloudflare; do it now, whether or not a busy stretch is coming.

The cache rule

In Cloudflare: your site → Caching → Cache Rules → Create rule, named "Ghost pages". Choose Edit expression and paste, with your site's name on the first line:

(http.host eq "example.com"
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/ghost/")
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/members/")
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/.ghost/")
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/.site/")
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/.puppy/")
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/.well-known/")
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/p/")
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/r/")
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/email/")
 and not starts_with(http.request.uri.path, "/unsubscribe")
 and http.request.uri.path ne "/live-status.json"
 and not http.cookie contains "ghost-members-ssr"
 and not http.cookie contains "ghost-admin-api-session")

Then: Cache eligibility: Eligible for cache. Edge TTL: Ignore cache-control header and use this TTL, 5 minutes. Browser TTL: Respect origin (browsers keep asking, and Cloudflare answers).

What's left out, and why: Ghost Admin and its API (/ghost/, the Social preview too), members' sign-in and account (/members/, and anyone with a members' cookie, who may see paid posts), Ghost's analytics (/.ghost/), the theme's clicks and a mascot's pets (/.site/, /.puppy/), previews (/p/), the newsletter's links (/r/, /email/, /unsubscribe), ActivityPub (/.well-known/) and the LIVE NOW bar's status (/live-status.json, which must be fresh).

What changes for you

  • A new or edited post can take up to 5 minutes to show for visitors who aren't signed in. You see it at once while signed in to Ghost. In a hurry: Caching → Configuration → Purge Everything.
  • To check it works: curl -sI https://example.com/ | grep -i cf-cache-status says MISS, then HIT on a second try.
  • Ghost's own visitor numbers keep counting: the page view is sent by the visitor's browser, not by the page's request.

Which cloud, and what it costs

Both work the same way from here on. GKE is cheaper for a short stretch: Google's free tier covers one zonal cluster's fee, and its nodes cost less. EKS is the one to choose if you already use AWS.

These are rough list prices from mid-2026, for planning only: check each cloud's pricing calculator before a long stretch. The part that varies is traffic. Cloudflare's cache takes most of the pages and pictures, but not the live stream: 320 kbps is about 144 MB per listener per hour, so a three-hour show with 100 listeners is about 43 GB, or $4–5.

What keeps costing after a stretch: the databases, the bucket and the image registry, until you delete them. A stopped RDS database starts again by itself after 7 days.

Amazon EKS, once

You need the aws CLI (v2, signed in: aws sts get-caller-identity), eksctl, kubectl and helm (only for the load balancer controller). The commands use these names; NS is your site's short name (ID_PREFIX), which is also its namespace on the cluster:

export AWS_REGION=us-east-1 CLUSTER=creator-kit NS=mysite
export ACCOUNT=$(aws sts get-caller-identity --query Account --output text)

The cluster

Check k8s/clusters/eks.yaml first: its name and region (the ones above), the Kubernetes version and the two m7i.xlarge nodes. Then (about 20 minutes):

eksctl create cluster -f k8s/clusters/eks.yaml
kubectl apply -f k8s/clusters/eks-gp3.yaml          # the gp3 disks every volume asks for
kubectl config current-context                       # should name the new cluster

The AWS Load Balancer Controller makes the load balancers the site asks for. Its ServiceAccount and IAM role came with the cluster:

helm repo add eks https://aws.github.io/eks-charts && helm repo update
helm install aws-load-balancer-controller eks/aws-load-balancer-controller -n kube-system \
  --set clusterName=$CLUSTER --set serviceAccount.create=false \
  --set serviceAccount.name=aws-load-balancer-controller
kubectl -n kube-system rollout status deploy/aws-load-balancer-controller

Databases (RDS)

Both go in the cluster's network, on its private subnets, and only the cluster's nodes may reach them:

VPC=$(aws eks describe-cluster --name $CLUSTER --query cluster.resourcesVpcConfig.vpcId --output text)
NODE_SG=$(aws eks describe-cluster --name $CLUSTER --query cluster.resourcesVpcConfig.clusterSecurityGroupId --output text)
SUBNETS=$(aws ec2 describe-subnets --filters Name=vpc-id,Values=$VPC Name=tag:kubernetes.io/role/internal-elb,Values=1 \
  --query 'Subnets[].SubnetId' --output text)
aws rds create-db-subnet-group --db-subnet-group-name $NS --db-subnet-group-description $NS --subnet-ids $SUBNETS
DB_SG=$(aws ec2 create-security-group --group-name $NS-db --description "$NS databases" --vpc-id $VPC --query GroupId --output text)
aws ec2 authorize-security-group-ingress --group-id $DB_SG --protocol tcp --port 3306 --source-group $NODE_SG
aws ec2 authorize-security-group-ingress --group-id $DB_SG --protocol tcp --port 5432 --source-group $NODE_SG

# Postiz and Temporal connect without TLS (the traffic stays inside the cluster's network)
aws rds create-db-parameter-group --db-parameter-group-name $NS-pg17 --db-parameter-group-family postgres17 \
  --description "no forced TLS"
aws rds modify-db-parameter-group --db-parameter-group-name $NS-pg17 \
  --parameters ParameterName=rds.force_ssl,ParameterValue=0,ApplyMethod=immediate

aws rds create-db-instance --db-instance-identifier $NS-mysql --engine mysql --engine-version 8.0 \
  --db-instance-class db.t4g.small --allocated-storage 20 --storage-type gp3 --db-name ghost \
  --master-username admin --master-user-password '<a new password>' \
  --db-subnet-group-name $NS --vpc-security-group-ids $DB_SG --no-publicly-accessible --backup-retention-period 1
aws rds create-db-instance --db-instance-identifier $NS-postgres --engine postgres --engine-version 17 \
  --db-instance-class db.t4g.small --allocated-storage 20 --storage-type gp3 --db-parameter-group-name $NS-pg17 \
  --master-username postgres --master-user-password '<another new password>' \
  --db-subnet-group-name $NS --vpc-security-group-ids $DB_SG --no-publicly-accessible --backup-retention-period 1
aws rds wait db-instance-available --db-instance-identifier $NS-mysql
aws rds wait db-instance-available --db-instance-identifier $NS-postgres
aws rds describe-db-instances --query 'DBInstances[].[DBInstanceIdentifier,Endpoint.Address]' --output table

Keep both passwords and both addresses: they go in the cluster's folder. Ghost's database is called ghost here. If the Compose side's has another name, that's fine: the migration reads each side's from its own settings.

The bucket and Ghost's role

Ghost's pictures, media and files go in a private bucket. Ghost serves them itself at the usual /content/… addresses, so no link changes. Ghost reaches the bucket through an IAM role tied to its Kubernetes ServiceAccount (IRSA), so no key is stored anywhere:

BUCKET=$NS-content-$ACCOUNT                       # bucket names are global: this one is yours
aws s3api create-bucket --bucket $BUCKET           # outside us-east-1: --create-bucket-configuration LocationConstraint=$AWS_REGION
aws s3api put-public-access-block --bucket $BUCKET --public-access-block-configuration \
  BlockPublicAcls=true,IgnorePublicAcls=true,BlockPublicPolicy=true,RestrictPublicBuckets=true
cat > ghost-s3.json <<EOF
{"Version": "2012-10-17", "Statement": [
  {"Effect": "Allow", "Action": ["s3:GetObject", "s3:PutObject", "s3:DeleteObject"], "Resource": "arn:aws:s3:::$BUCKET/*"},
  {"Effect": "Allow", "Action": "s3:ListBucket", "Resource": "arn:aws:s3:::$BUCKET"}]}
EOF
aws iam create-policy --policy-name $NS-ghost-s3 --policy-document file://ghost-s3.json
eksctl create iamserviceaccount --cluster $CLUSTER --namespace $NS --name ghost --role-name $NS-ghost \
  --attach-policy-arn arn:aws:iam::$ACCOUNT:policy/$NS-ghost-s3 --role-only --approve
echo arn:aws:iam::$ACCOUNT:role/$NS-ghost         # GHOST_ROLE_ARN

--role-only: the kit makes the ServiceAccount itself, with this role's name on it. The role trusts only ServiceAccount ghost in your namespace. The migration's helper pod uses it too, to fill the bucket.

The image registry (ECR)

Only for images you build yourself (the Setup guide's The images); a site on a released kit uses the release's:

for i in tb-setup icecast-relay ghost-bridge; do aws ecr create-repository --repository-name creator-kit/$i; done
aws ecr get-login-password | docker login --username AWS --password-stdin $ACCOUNT.dkr.ecr.$AWS_REGION.amazonaws.com
echo $ACCOUNT.dkr.ecr.$AWS_REGION.amazonaws.com/creator-kit     # the registry for build-images.sh

The nodes may pull from it already: eksctl gives them read access to ECR.

Google GKE, once

You need gcloud (signed in: gcloud auth login), kubectl and the GKE auth plugin (gcloud components install gke-gcloud-auth-plugin), and a project with billing on. NS is your site's short name, as above:

export PROJECT=<your project> REGION=us-central1 NS=mysite
gcloud config set project $PROJECT

The cluster

GKE Standard, one zone, two e2-standard-4 nodes, Workload Identity on (about 10 minutes). It also turns on the APIs it needs and points kubectl at the new cluster:

PROJECT=$PROJECT REGION=$REGION k8s/clusters/gke.sh up

The volumes use standard-rwo, GKE's own disk class, so there's nothing more to set up for them.

Databases (Cloud SQL)

Pods reach them through the Cloud SQL Auth Proxy, which runs beside each pod that needs a database and signs in as a Google service account. Nothing is opened to the internet, even though the instances have public addresses.

gcloud sql instances create $NS-mysql --database-version MYSQL_8_0 --edition enterprise \
  --tier db-g1-small --region $REGION --storage-size 20 --storage-type SSD
gcloud sql users set-password root --host % --instance $NS-mysql --password '<a new password>'
gcloud sql databases create ghost --instance $NS-mysql

gcloud sql instances create $NS-postgres --database-version POSTGRES_17 --edition enterprise \
  --tier db-g1-small --region $REGION --storage-size 20 --storage-type SSD
gcloud sql users set-password postgres --instance $NS-postgres --password '<another new password>'

gcloud sql instances list --format 'table(name,connectionName)'     # MYSQL_INSTANCE, POSTGRES_INSTANCE

# the service account the proxies act as, and its link to Kubernetes ServiceAccount "cloud-sql"
gcloud iam service-accounts create $NS-sql
SQL_SA=$NS-sql@$PROJECT.iam.gserviceaccount.com
gcloud projects add-iam-policy-binding $PROJECT --member serviceAccount:$SQL_SA --role roles/cloudsql.client
gcloud iam service-accounts add-iam-policy-binding $SQL_SA --role roles/iam.workloadIdentityUser \
  --member "serviceAccount:$PROJECT.svc.id.goog[$NS/cloud-sql]"
echo $SQL_SA                                                        # CLOUD_SQL_SA

The bucket and its key

Ghost uses the same adapter as on AWS, through Cloud Storage's S3-compatible side, with an HMAC key belonging to a service account that may only use this bucket:

BUCKET=$NS-content-$PROJECT
gcloud storage buckets create gs://$BUCKET --location $REGION --uniform-bucket-level-access --public-access-prevention
gcloud iam service-accounts create $NS-storage
gcloud storage buckets add-iam-policy-binding gs://$BUCKET \
  --member serviceAccount:$NS-storage@$PROJECT.iam.gserviceaccount.com --role roles/storage.objectUser
gcloud storage hmac create $NS-storage@$PROJECT.iam.gserviceaccount.com   # prints accessId and secret: keep both

The image registry (Artifact Registry)

Only for images you build yourself:

gcloud artifacts repositories create creator-kit --repository-format docker --location $REGION
gcloud auth configure-docker $REGION-docker.pkg.dev
echo $REGION-docker.pkg.dev/$PROJECT/creator-kit              # the registry for build-images.sh

If pods later say ImagePullBackOff with "permission denied", the nodes' service account can't read the registry: give it roles/artifactregistry.reader (newer projects no longer grant it by default).

The cluster's folder

The cluster gets a folder of its own, a copy of the Compose site's settings and secrets, made once and kept for every stretch (it holds secrets: never anywhere that syncs or gets committed). How to make it: the first step of the Operations guide's Compose → Kubernetes. Then, for a cloud:

site.env

SettingIn the cloud
TRUSTED_EXTRAEmpty, but for your own address while you try it out: home-network ranges mean nothing there, and could even match the cloud's own addresses.
NTFY_URLAn ntfy server your phone reaches from anywhere, like https://ntfy.sh, if the Compose site uses one at home. With ntfy.sh, use a long topic nobody could guess.
GRAFANA_HOST, GRAFANA_URLThe cluster has its own Grafana, at grafana.<your site>: a home-only address won't do.
DATA_DIRNot used by the cluster: the Compose site's data folder is given to the migration scripts instead.

Leave ID_PREFIX as it is: the scripts find the site by it on both sides, and it keeps the dashboards' history continuous.

Its secrets

The ones the Setup guide lists in The site's secrets for a cloud: in secrets/ghost.env, Ghost's login on the cloud's MySQL (EKS: admin, its password and the database ghost; GKE: root, its password and ghost); secrets/cloud.env with the Postgres master login; secrets/s3.env with the HMAC key (GKE only); and secrets/ghost-mail.env, Ghost's mail, without which its sign-in codes and members' links won't arrive. The rest stay as the Compose site had them: the data that moves was made with them. A new NTFY_TOPIC goes in both secrets/postiz.env and secrets/monitoring.env, so the alerts reach your phone too.

The cloud's addresses

From the setup above, in the overlay's file (the comments in it say what each line is):

cp k8s/overlays/eks/cloud.env.example kit/sites/mysite-k8s/cloud-eks.env     # MYSQL_HOST, POSTGRES_HOST, S3_BUCKET, S3_REGION, GHOST_ROLE_ARN
cp k8s/overlays/gke/cloud.env.example kit/sites/mysite-k8s/cloud-gke.env     # MYSQL_INSTANCE, POSTGRES_INSTANCE, CLOUD_SQL_SA, S3_BUCKET

Then check it renders, with no cluster needed: k8s/up.sh kit/sites/mysite-k8s eks --render (or gke) writes everything the cluster would get to data/k8s/rendered.yaml in the folder (mode 600: it holds the secrets).

First up

A day or so before the move, while the Compose site keeps serving: the cluster gets the site with empty databases. This checks everything only a real cloud can: the permissions, the load balancer, the database rules and the certificates.

  1. Let yourself through Cloudflare-only for the test: curl -s https://ifconfig.me, then TRUSTED_EXTRA=<that>/32 in the folder's site.env.
  2. Up. It asks before using kubectl's current cluster, so check the name it shows. The first time takes a while (pulling images, the analytics' first start), so give it longer: 
    UP_TIMEOUT=1200 k8s/up.sh kit/sites/mysite-k8s eks        # or gke
    It stops by itself on a pod that's stuck, and says why. In another terminal, kubectl -n mysite get pods -w shows them come up; Ghost, Grafana and the bridge wait in CreateContainerConfigError until the analytics' setup has made its tokens, which is normal.
  3. The load balancer's address: kubectl -n mysite get svc traefik tinyice-source (EXTERNAL-IP: a hostname on EKS, an address on GKE).
  4. The site through the cluster rather than Cloudflare, as the Setup guide's Test before the DNS says (-k until the certificate arrives, a minute or two). For a browser, a hosts-file line with the address (on EKS, dig +short <the hostname>) and the site's names: example.com, postiz., grafana. and live.. Take it out afterwards.
  5. In that browser, make a throwaway owner at https://example.com/ghost/, and upload a picture to a draft: if it shows, Ghost can reach the bucket. The move replaces this owner and draft.
  6. Grafana at grafana.example.com (user admin, the password in secrets/monitoring.env): the Health dashboard has numbers.
  7. Park the bridge until the move, so it sends no notifications from an empty site: kubectl -n mysite scale deploy/postiz-ghost-bridge --replicas 0. The move's own up.sh brings it back.

Anything wrong: Troubleshooting. The first time on each cloud, also go through what to watch.

The move

About half an hour, most of it copying. The Compose site keeps serving visitors until the DNS moves. Anything written there after the copy starts is lost (Ghost Admin edits, new members, comments), so pick a quiet moment and keep the gap short.

  • Before: freeze Ghost Admin (no edits or new posts until the DNS has moved), nothing scheduled to publish in the next hour, and no live show during the move. Note down your DNS records for the site, www, postiz and live: they go back when you come home.
  • The move itself is the Operations guide's Compose → Kubernetes: to-cloud.sh's dry run, then the real one, then a check of the site through the cluster's own address (sign in to Ghost Admin, too: a new-device code arrives by mail if secrets/ghost-mail.env is right).

Point the DNS at the cloud

  1. Take your address back out: TRUSTED_EXTRA= in site.env, then k8s/up.sh again (Traefik restarts, Ghost doesn't). Take the hosts-file line out.
  2. In Cloudflare's DNS, point the site, www, postiz and live at the cluster, all proxied (orange): on EKS a CNAME to traefik's hostname (Cloudflare allows one at the root), on GKE an A record to its address. Add grafana the same way. Proxied records switch within seconds.
  3. For your streaming software: a DNS-only (grey) record, like source.example.com, to tinyice-source's address, since Cloudflare doesn't carry the stream's port. The software's server becomes that name, port 8222, with the same mount and password. Keep the port to your own addresses: LIVE_SOURCE_RANGES in site.env (the Setup guide's Live), then k8s/up.sh again.
  4. Check from your phone, off Wi-Fi: the site, a post, the LIVE NOW bar. Then end the freeze.

Coming home

The same freeze, the other way: the Operations guide's Kubernetes → Compose. to-nas.sh brings back what was made in the cloud (posts, members, uploads, the bridge's history, page views and the dashboards' numbers), keeping what the Compose side had first. Then:

  • Put your DNS records back as you noted them, and delete grafana's and the stream source's. The streaming software goes back to its usual server.
  • Check from your phone, then end the freeze. The phone's subscription goes back to the usual topic, if you changed it.

Tearing down

Only once the site is home and checked. Deleting the cluster deletes its volumes too.

AWS

k8s/up.sh kit/sites/mysite-k8s eks --down       # the site first, so its load balancers go before the cluster
eksctl delete cluster -f k8s/clusters/eks.yaml
aws rds delete-db-instance --db-instance-identifier $NS-mysql --skip-final-snapshot
aws rds delete-db-instance --db-instance-identifier $NS-postgres --skip-final-snapshot
aws s3 rb s3://$BUCKET --force

Google Cloud

k8s/up.sh kit/sites/mysite-k8s gke --down
PROJECT=$PROJECT k8s/clusters/gke.sh down
gcloud sql instances delete $NS-mysql
gcloud sql instances delete $NS-postgres
gcloud storage rm --recursive gs://$BUCKET

Keeping things for the next stretch? The databases are the main cost: stop them (RDS starts a stopped one again after 7 days) or delete them, since the next move fills new ones anyway. The image registry, the roles, the service accounts and the cluster's folder cost nothing, or close to it. Check the billing page a day later to see nothing was left running.

Troubleshooting

Start with kubectl -n mysite get pods, and the Operations guide's Kubernetes troubleshooting. In a cloud, also:

ImagePullBackOff with images you built
"no match for platform": built on an ARM computer (a recent Mac, say) without --platform linux/amd64: the clouds' nodes here are Intel or AMD. "unauthorized" or "denied": the nodes can't read the registry (on GKE, see the registry).
Pods Pending: "unbound PersistentVolumeClaims"
EKS: kubectl get sc lists gp3 (eks-gp3.yaml), and the EBS CSI add-on is running? GKE has standard-rwo already. "Insufficient memory": add a node (EKS's maxSize allows 3).
Ghost keeps restarting: "ER_ACCESS_DENIED", or "ECONNREFUSED"/"ETIMEDOUT"
Access denied: secrets/ghost.env's login (secrets/puppi3.env in a folder made before kit 1.2.0). Can't connect: on EKS, the database security group (port 3306 from the nodes' group) and MYSQL_HOST; on GKE, the proxy's log (-c cloud-sql-proxy) and MYSQL_INSTANCE.
Postiz or Temporal wait forever in Init:
The postgres-logins Job's log: kubectl -n mysite logs job/postgres-logins. Usually secrets/cloud.env's master login, or (EKS) the security group's port 5432.
Cloudflare shows 521 or 522
Cloudflare can't reach the cluster: does the DNS record point at traefik's address (not tinyice-source's), and is kubectl -n mysite get pods -l app=traefik Running?
Everything says 403
Cloudflare-only is turning you away. Before the DNS moved: your address isn't in TRUSTED_EXTRA, or it has changed. After: "keeps the visitor's address" in the first time.
Certificate warnings that don't go away
Traefik's log: kubectl -n mysite logs deploy/traefik | grep -i acme. Usually CLOUDFLARE_DNS_API_TOKEN in secrets/traefik.env.
No mail from Ghost
secrets/ghost-mail.env is there and right? kubectl -n mysite exec deploy/ghost -c ghost -- printenv | grep ^mail__, then Ghost's log when you ask for a sign-in code.

Command reference

S=kit/sites/mysite-k8s
k8s/up.sh $S eks --render                      # what would be applied, no cluster (data/k8s/rendered.yaml)
k8s/up.sh $S eks                               # apply and wait (asks first; UP_TIMEOUT=<s> for a slow first start)
k8s/up.sh $S eks --down                        # the site off the cluster (name the overlay: alone, --down means the local test cluster's)
k8s/migrate/to-cloud.sh $S eks --from <user>@<host>:/path/to/mysite/data [-n]
k8s/migrate/to-nas.sh   $S eks --to   <user>@<host>:/path/to/mysite/data [-n] [--no-start]
kubectl -n mysite get pods                     # -w to watch
kubectl -n mysite logs deploy/ghost -c ghost   # -c cloud-sql-proxy on GKE; --previous after a restart
kubectl -n mysite get svc traefik tinyice-source
kubectl -n mysite scale deploy/tinyice-relay --replicas 4
kubectl -n mysite port-forward deploy/tinyice 8080:8080

Use gke in place of eks on Google Cloud, and your own short name in place of mysite.
